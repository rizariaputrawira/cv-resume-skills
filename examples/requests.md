# Example requests

These are invocation examples, not real candidate records or measured hiring outcomes. Supply your own authorized files in a private workspace; do not paste personal or proprietary material into a public issue.

## Industry résumé from notes

> Read `skills/resume-writing/SKILL.md` and follow it. Help turn my notes in `.local/profile.md` into a US software-engineering résumé in English. Start from my facts, ask about material missing details, and do not invent numbers. Deliver Markdown for my review. I do not have a job posting yet.

Expected result: a general role-focused draft, factual questions, and honest verification limits—not a fabricated job-specific match or an asserted rendered page count.

## Tailor to a supplied posting

> Use resume-writing with `.local/resume.md` and `.local/job.md`. Tailor the résumé to the job, show a separate requirement-to-evidence table, preserve my official titles, and identify unknown requirements. The employer requires DOCX under its stated size limit. Do not submit anything.

Expected result: supported content and a gap analysis; an actual inspected DOCX if tools are available. If export is unavailable, the draft remains useful but the requested DOCX must be reported as missing, not completed.

## Review without changing files

> Use resume-writing to review `.local/resume.pdf` for readability, evidence, and ATS parsing risks. Report prioritized findings and exact suggested changes. Do not rewrite files, upload the document, or claim an ATS test unless a real authorized parser was exercised.

Expected result: findings and suggestions only; observed checks separated from unavailable checks.

## Academic CV for a teaching position

> Read `skills/academic-cv/SKILL.md` and follow it. Use `.local/academic-notes.md` and `.local/faculty-call.md` to draft an English CV for a teaching-focused faculty application. Preserve publication statuses and author order, distinguish my TA work from course ownership, and ask before including referee contacts. Deliver Markdown for my review.

Expected result: a scholarly CV with relevant teaching emphasis, accurate bibliographic categories, no industry one-page limit, and separate open questions.

## Ambiguous “CV” request

> Help me write a CV. I haven't specified whether this is for a company job or an academic application.

Expected result: resolve purpose before imposing either document type. The word “CV” alone does not determine the format.

## Translation

> Use resume-writing to translate `.local/resume-en.md` into professional Indonesian for the Jakarta role described in `.local/job-id.md`. Preserve my qualifications and achievements without inventing local degree equivalencies. Ask if a destination-specific convention materially changes the document.

Expected result: natural target-language editing, unchanged facts, and no fabricated proficiency or credentials. Locale-specific requirements still need evidence.
