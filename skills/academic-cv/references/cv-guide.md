# Academic CV guide

## Source scope

[Harvard MCS's GSAS guide](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/gsas-cv-cover-letter-guide-2/) describes a CV as a record of academic accomplishments used for academic positions, fellowships, grants, and some research roles beyond academia. It says there is no single best format, to follow disciplinary conventions, and that most graduate-student CVs are two to five pages. That observation is not a universal length requirement or permission to ignore a call's page limit.

[Berkeley's academic CV elements guide](https://career.berkeley.edu/grad-students-postdocs/academic-job-search/the-cv-part-2-elements/) likewise treats sections as modules chosen and ordered for the audience, and recommends disciplinary review. It is the supporting source here for explicitly distinguishing submitted work and forthcoming publications. Do not attribute every rule in this reference to Harvard.

## Select sections instead of enforcing a template

| Section | Include when relevant and evidenced |
| --- | --- |
| Name and professional contact | Supplied identity, email, relevant location/contact and scholarly links such as ORCID; no invented identifiers |
| Education | Actual degree/institution/field, awarded or expected date; thesis/dissertation and advisor when relevant |
| Appointments | Accurate titles, institutions, dates, and appointment types; preserve concurrent or visiting roles |
| Research experience/interests | Specific contributions, methods, and research direction; distinguish completed work from future interests |
| Publications / scholarly outputs | Accurate author order, titles, venues, dates, output types, and visible publication statuses |
| Presentations | Title, event, date, and venue as supplied; distinguish invited talks, contributed talks, posters, and scheduled future work |
| Teaching and mentoring | Course, institution, dates, level and actual responsibilities; separate teaching assistant from instructor of record |
| Funding, fellowships, honors | Awarding body, title, date, amount/currency if supported and useful, and actual recipient/role |
| Service and leadership | Departmental, institutional, disciplinary, community or editorial work, with role and dates |
| Skills and languages | Relevant methods/tools and accurately described proficiency; no inferred fluency |
| References | Only as appropriate to the call and field, with consent and current details; see the reference branch below |

Education commonly follows contact information for early-career academics, but there is no rigid order for every stage. Research-heavy and teaching-heavy applications should emphasize different evidence. Use reverse chronology within sections unless a field-specific bibliography convention or required template directs otherwise. Omit empty categories rather than inserting “none,” fabricated entries, or placeholders.

A full private archival CV and a targeted application CV have different purposes. Tailoring may reorder, condense, or select; it does not authorize deleting the archival record. If a bibliography is selected rather than complete, label it “Selected Publications” so the reader is not misled.

## Publication integrity

Read this branch whenever publications, preprints, or works in progress appear.

| Candidate-supported status | Safe treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Published | Full citation in the appropriate publication/output section |
| Accepted, not yet published | “Accepted,” “in press,” or “forthcoming” as supported by acceptance and field convention; do not invent publication year/pages |
| Preprint | Label as preprint with the supplied repository/identifier; not proof of journal acceptance or peer review |
| Submitted / under review | Clearly labeled submitted or under-review work, separately grouped when appropriate; not accepted or forthcoming |
| In preparation / work in progress | Label visibly as work in progress, in a separate relevant section if the field/call allows it |
| Rejected / withdrawn | Do not retain an accepted/under-review label; clarify current status and use a truthful category only if relevant |

Berkeley explicitly reserves “forthcoming” for accepted work, not merely submitted or under review. An invitation to revise is not unconditional acceptance. Preserve coauthor sequence and any verified equal-contribution designation. Do not promote the candidate to first author, treat a workshop abstract as a journal article, or count duplicate preprint/journal versions as distinct published works without clear explanation.

Use the discipline's citation style consistently. DOI/URL lookup can improve a citation if authorized and supported by the actual publication record; it must not become a guessed identifier or an unverified claim. Omit missing optional metadata rather than fabricate it. Separate bibliographic citations inside the CV from research-source citations belonging in the working notes.

## Contribution and award integrity

Use the candidate's actual role. “Supported data collection for a funded project” is not “secured the grant.” Distinguish total project funding from the amount awarded personally. Preserve amount, currency, award period, and team role; omit an unknown amount. Do not invent selectivity, prestige, evaluation scores, citation metrics, or student-learning improvements.

Describe teaching scope: led sections, marked assignments, designed lessons, delivered lectures, or served as instructor of record only when true. “Teaching interests” or “prepared to teach” is not past teaching experience. Future appointments, talks, or degrees need explicit scheduled/expected qualifiers.

## References and personal information

Read this branch when the call requests referees or the candidate supplies referee details.

Unlike a US industry résumé, an academic CV may include references or a separate reference sheet. Harvard GSAS includes references among basic areas; Berkeley discusses a separate page. Follow the call and the field. Confirm that each referee has agreed to serve and the candidate authorizes including the supplied, current professional details. If either is unknown, leave the details out, ask the candidate, and record the unresolved requirement separately. Do not contact referees by implication.

Avoid unnecessary demographic or sensitive information. A full home address, date of birth, marital status, photograph, or national identifier is not a default input. Locale and application requirements may differ; clarify them rather than copying a US or another country's convention globally.

## Layout and checks

Harvard GSAS suggests common fonts, 10–12 pt text, equal 0.75–1 inch margins, restrained emphasis, and avoiding text boxes and shading. Treat these as that guide's recommendations, not a single official format for every academic discipline. Choose legibility, suitable paper size, and the call's limits; do not shrink a long scholarly record to one page just to imitate a résumé.

For multipage documents keep section headings with their content and citations intact where possible. Optional repeated name/page number aids page identification; primary contact details remain in body text. A simple reading order helps both people and extraction tools. A PDF can be text-based yet still map incorrectly into an application system.

Inspect actual final exports: all pages, text order, author names/diacritics, citation status labels, URLs, clipped lines, unintended blank pages, and compliance with required size/length. If tools or access are missing, report exactly which checks and exports were not done. Do not claim “ATS certified” or academic-selection success.
