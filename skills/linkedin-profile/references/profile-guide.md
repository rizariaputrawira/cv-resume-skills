# LinkedIn profile guidance

## Intake and source limits

Ask for only the fields needed. Pasted profile sections, redacted screenshots, a résumé/CV, and candidate notes are enough for a useful draft. If the user wants a local copy of their own profile, LinkedIn documents [Save to PDF](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a541960) under **More** or **Resources** on desktop. Availability and English-language constraints apply; use pasted text if it is missing or unreliable. A PDF is not proof of live visibility, a complete section inventory, the mobile layout, or the current state of Featured, notification, and availability settings.

Do not collect profiles through scraping or work around a login wall. LinkedIn's [User Agreement, §8.2](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement#dos) restricts automated scraping and circumvention. Prefer material the person chooses to provide. Platform help can be checked without sending identifying profile details or confidential draft content to a search engine.

## Write for one primary reader

Pick a primary audience for this draft: recruiters for a role family, prospective clients, or an academic/professional audience. Name what the person wants readers to understand or do. Priorities depend on the gap: incorrect work history comes before headline polish; the strongest relevant public artifact may matter more than filling every optional section. These are editorial choices, not platform weights.

| Section | Write or assess |
| --- | --- |
| Headline | Lead with a supported role/function and focus. If changing careers, distinguish the current background from the intended direction. Put the meaningful phrase first; do not depend on a fixed visible preview length. |
| About | Write a few short first-person paragraphs in the person's voice: what they do, relevant evidence or method, and an optional next step they are comfortable making public. Put the main point in the opening lines. Do not paste a résumé summary or append a keyword list. |
| Experience | Keep actual title, employer, and dates. Explain role scope and one or more specific contributions; use clear bullets or short prose. Identify personal versus team work and keep results qualified. Separate job, volunteer, academic, and independent work. |
| Skills | Prioritize relevant, demonstrable terms and connect them to actual work or education where useful. Preserve proficiency limits and use recognizable names, not synonyms repeated for density. |
| Education, credentials, publications | Include relevant supported items. Preserve degree completion, credential status, authorship, and published/preprint/under-review distinctions. Skip private or uncleared material. |
| Featured | Select only public/shareable, user-authorized artifacts. For each, propose order, title, what it demonstrates, and what access the intended viewer needs. If there is no cleared asset, recommend leaving it out instead of inventing one. |
| Photo, banner, URL | Address only when requested or relevant. Assess supplied visuals or observations, not a missing image. Suggest checking crop and readability on desktop and mobile; do not judge appearance or promise a view increase. Suggest making the URL easy to share only if the user wants that change. |

LinkedIn's [profile tip sheet](https://socialimpact.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/socialimpact-linkedin-com/lifg-resources/linkedin-training-content/linkedin-profile-tip-sheet.pdf) recommends short first-person About text, concrete Experience, skill context, and Featured work samples. [Recruiter Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a414428) describes Job Titles from Experience and Skills from explicit skills and relevant profile sections. Neither establishes a ranking bonus for a headline, pinned skill count, or a keyword-density target.

### Synthetic example: role transition

Evidence: a retail inventory coordinator built a weekly stock report with spreadsheet pivots and completed a SQL course; they are exploring data analyst roles. A suitable headline is “Inventory Coordinator | Stock Reporting | Building SQL Skills.” Do not call them an employed Data Analyst or imply they used SQL in production. In About, distinguish reporting work from coursework. Do not label a class exercise a client project.

### Featured review

A public repository, article, poster, or presentation can be useful if it demonstrates the person's own contribution and is cleared to share. For a team repository, say what the person contributed; a link is not proof of authorship of the whole project. Do not feature internal dashboards, unapproved client decks, leaked data, or private files. Check the intended viewer's access only when explicitly authorized and possible without private access; otherwise give the user a manual link/preview check. LinkedIn's [Featured FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a553419) distinguishes Featured from search discoverability: its contents are not discoverable through search, viewers must be signed in, and visibility can depend on the underlying content.

## Visibility and manual publishing

Read this branch when the user asks about privacy, job-search signals, or publishing edits.

- [Open to Work](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508): the public option includes people at the current employer. LinkedIn says it takes steps to hide the recruiter-only signal from recruiters at that company but cannot guarantee complete privacy. If employer discovery is unacceptable, recommend leaving recruiter/public signals off rather than promising secrecy. Keep location and employment history accurate; use actual job preferences rather than substituting a misleading location.
- [Public profile controls](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a528138): review eligibility of sections for viewing outside LinkedIn separately from what signed-in people can see. Search engines can take weeks or months to reflect changes. Do not claim a removal erases older copies or cached results.
- [Share profile updates](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a529062): notification controls and visibility are different. Saved profile changes can be visible to people who view the profile even if network notifications are off. Do not claim a quiet edit is invisible.
- Verify text limits, any mobile truncation, and current editor options at publishing time. Do not assume limits quoted by a third-party skill apply to every account, field, locale, or interface. Give the user the copy and checklist; they make the changes themselves.
